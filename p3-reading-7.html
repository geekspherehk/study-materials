🏃 The Sports Day

P3 英文阅读理解 · R7 · 故事类

📖 阅读短文
📚 故事 Fiction

The morning of Sports Day dawned bright and clear. Colourful banners fluttered across the school playground, and the loudspeaker blasted energetic music. Students from every class gathered on the field, wearing their house team colours — red, blue, yellow, and green. The air was electric with excitement.

Tom was in the yellow house. He had been practising for weeks for the 100-metre sprint. Every evening after school, he ran laps around the basketball court while his father timed him with a stopwatch. Tom was not the fastest runner in his class — Leo from the red house was much quicker — but Tom wanted to beat his personal best. "You are not competing against others," his father always said. "You are competing against yourself."

The first event was the relay race. Four runners from each house had to pass a baton without dropping it. Tom was the third runner. When the baton reached his hands, his heart pounded like a drum. He gripped the baton tightly and sprinted with all his might. He could hear his classmates cheering from the sidelines. He passed the baton smoothly to the last runner, and the yellow house finished second. It was a good start.

Next came the long jump and the sack race. The yellow house team performed well in both. By lunchtime, the red house was in the lead, followed closely by yellow and blue. During the break, Tom felt nervous about the 100-metre sprint. His legs felt wobbly. His best friend, Sam, put a hand on his shoulder. "Just do your best, Tom. That is all that matters."

The moment arrived. Tom stood at the starting line, his eyes fixed on the track ahead. The whistle blew. He exploded forward. For ten seconds, nothing existed except the wind in his ears and the blur of the ground beneath his feet. He crossed the finish line in third place, but when he looked at the scoreboard, he gasped — he had beaten his personal best by nearly a full second! His teammates lifted him onto their shoulders and cheered. Tom grinned from ear to ear. He had not won the gold medal, but he had won something even better: he had proved to himself that hard work pays off.
