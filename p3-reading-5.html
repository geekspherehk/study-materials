🖌️ The Magic Paintbrush

P3 英文阅读理解 · R5 · 故事类

📖 阅读短文
📚 故事 Fiction

Maya lived in a tiny apartment above her grandmother's noodle shop. She loved to draw, but her family could not afford proper art supplies. Maya used old newspapers and a worn-out pencil stub. Still, she never stopped practising. She drew cats, dragons, and fantastical castles on every scrap of paper she could find.

One rainy afternoon, a peculiar old woman walked into the noodle shop. She wore a tattered cloak and carried a bamboo case. After finishing her bowl of noodles, she beckoned Maya over. "I have watched you draw through the window," the woman said with a mysterious smile. "You have a gift. Take this paintbrush — it is magical." She handed Maya a paintbrush with bristles that shimmered like silver. Before Maya could thank her, the woman vanished into the rain.

That evening, Maya dipped the brush into water and painted a butterfly on a napkin. To her astonishment, the butterfly fluttered its wings and flew off the napkin! It circled around the room, leaving a trail of sparkling dust. Maya gasped. The paintbrush truly was magic — everything she painted came to life!

Maya was tempted to paint expensive things — gold coins, a huge house, a car. But she remembered how kind her grandmother was, always giving free bowls of noodles to hungry neighbours. Instead, Maya painted fresh vegetables for the soup, a warm coat for the beggar on the corner, and a bouquet of flowers for the lonely old man next door. Each painting became real and brought joy to someone.

Word spread about the mysterious girl who could paint miracles. A greedy merchant heard the story and demanded Maya paint him a mountain of gold. Maya refused. The merchant became furious and tried to steal the paintbrush. But the moment his fingers touched it, the brush crumbled into dust. The magic was gone. Maya was sad, but she realised something important: the real magic was not the brush — it was her kind heart. She continued to draw with ordinary pencils, and her pictures, though still, were full of love.
